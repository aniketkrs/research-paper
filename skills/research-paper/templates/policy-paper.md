## Skill-specific instructions

1. **Issue summary + Key messages** are the most-read parts. Make them
   self-contained: a busy policymaker should be able to read just these and
   know what to do.
2. **Quantify whenever possible.** "Reduces emissions" is weak; "reduces
   emissions by an estimated 12 % (95 % CI 8–16 %) by 2035" is strong.
3. The **policy options comparison table** (§3) is mandatory — readers
   want to see alternatives evaluated, not just the recommendation.
4. **Equity analysis** (§4.3) is increasingly expected and often missing —
   include it.
5. **Cost–benefit analysis** with explicit assumptions; sensitivity analysis
   in the appendix for paper version.
6. **Implementation considerations** (§5) distinguish a serious policy
   paper from advocacy — show how the recommendation actually gets done.
7. **Sources** must be unimpeachable: government data, peer-reviewed studies,
   reputable international organizations. Avoid advocacy-group claims as
   primary evidence (use them for context, not facts).
8. Length: brief = 4–8 pages; full policy paper = 10–20 pages. Adjust depth
   accordingly.
